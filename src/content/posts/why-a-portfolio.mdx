I've put off a personal site for a long time.

Not because there was nothing to show — Doxa has been shipping for two years, FIDA is in production, I write specs, brand systems, and React Native screens every week. I put it off because a portfolio, done honestly, is a claim. It's a claim about what you can do, what you actually did, and who you were doing it with. In a small city like Bishoftu, where most software work happens through phone calls and WhatsApp threads, that claim is a public thing. You have to be willing to stand behind it.

So this site is my version of standing behind it.

## What "full loop" actually means

The line I use across the site is that UX is the whole loop — research, strategy, design, build, measure — not just the Figma file. That isn't marketing copy. It's the only way I've figured out how to ship product from here.

When I designed the FIDA customer app, the constraints weren't fonts and radii. The constraints were: a first-time user on a 4-year-old Android, on 3G, in a market with no brand recognition, trying to decide whether to type in a phone number that will later ring. That constraint reaches back through the design system to the copy to the pricing to the onboarding flow to the community survey we ran before drawing a screen. If I hand any one of those to someone else — the survey to a research vendor, the pricing to a consultant, the copy to a freelancer — the coherence breaks. The trust the user feels on screen four is a direct function of the choices made in phase one.

That's what "one operator across the loop" means. Not that I'm irreplaceable. That the loop *is* the design.

## What this notebook is for

Two things.

**Short posts, when there's something specific worth saying.** How I structure a Turborepo when the CMS has to be the source of truth. What actually moves adoption on a delivery app in a low-trust market. Why I stopped mocking the database in integration tests. The kind of note I wish I'd found when I was trying to figure it out.

**A working log.** Enough of a public trail that when I say "I shipped X," you can trace it. Case studies stay long; posts stay short; the /now page tells you what's on my desk this month.

I don't expect to write often. I ship more than I write, on purpose. But when I do write, it will be because a specific mistake or a specific pattern earned the essay.

If that's useful to you — a hiring team, a founder, another engineer trying to do this from a small city — good. That's the audience. Thanks for reading.
